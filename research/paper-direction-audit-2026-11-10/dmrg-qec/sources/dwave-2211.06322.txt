Recently, robust d-wave superconductive (SC) order has been unveiled in the ground state of the 2D t-t′-J
model — with both nearest-neighbor ( t) and next-nearest-neighbor ( t′) hoppings — by density matrix renor-
malization group studies. However, there is currently a debate on whether the d-wave SC holds up strong on
both t′/t > 0 and t′/t < 0 cases for the t-t′-J model, which correspond to the electron- and hole-doped sides
of the cuprate phase diagram, respectively. Here we exploit state-of-the-art thermal tensor network approach to
accurately obtain the phase diagram of the t-t′-J model on cylinders with widths up to W = 6 and down to
low temperature as T /J ≃ 0.06, pushing the boundaries of contemporary finite- T calculations. For t′/t > 0,
we find a domelike SC regime with a diverging d-wave pairing susceptibility, χSC ∝ 1/T α below a character-
istic temperature T ∗
c . Near optimal doping, T ∗
c reaches its highest value of about 0.15J. Above T ∗
c yet below
a higher crossover temperature T ∗, the magnetic susceptibility becomes suppressed, which can be related to
the onset of pseudogap (PG) behaviors. On the other hand, for t′/t < 0 we find the pairing correlations are
much weaker, although there exhibits a node-antinode structure in the PG regime as observed in the hole-doped
cuprates. The thermal tensor network calculations of the t-t′-J model underscore both the similarities and dif-
ferences in the finite-temperature phase diagram between the fundamental model and cuprates, yielding unique
insights into their intricate behaviors.
Introduction.— Understanding unconventional supercon-
ductive (SC) phase and the enigmatic normal states like those
in the pseudogap (PG) regime of the cuprate phase diagram
has become one of the major challenges in modern condensed
matter physics [1–6]. Theoretically, the two-dimensional (2D)
Hubbard [7, 8] and t-J models [9, 10] are believed to cap-
ture the essence of electron correlations [11, 12]. Despite ac-
tive and intensive studies [13–48], it remains elusive whether
these fundamental models can reproduce the d-wave SC and
PG states observed in the phase diagram.
Recently, great efforts have been devoted to the accu-
rate calculations of Hubbard and t-J models, significantly
advancing their understanding. It has been shown that in
the Hubbard model with only nearest-neighbor (NN) hop-
ping and a large repulsive U, long-range SC order is ab-
sent under 1/8 hole doping while a stripe order appears in-
stead [13, 15, 24, 29, 31, 36, 41]. Lately, by considering the
next NN hopping t′, large-scale density matrix renormaliza-
tion group (DMRG) studies revealed suppression of the stripe
order and the emergence of robustd-wave SC [37–39, 45–48].
Given the discoveries at zero temperature, it is natural to
inquire whether the t-t′-J model also contains exotic many-
electron states at elevated temperatures. There are compelling
questions to be answered, including whether the supercon-
ducting ground state expands to a SC phase at finite tempera-
ture; if yes, what is the characteristic temperature T ∗
c ? Does

and unbiased finite-temperature approach is essential.
While the finite-temperature Lanczos method is limited
within small system sizes [49–52] and the quantum Monte
Carlo approaches [19] suffer from the notorious sign prob-
lem at finite doping, the thermal tensor network (TN) pro-
vides a powerful framework to simulate correlated systems
at large scale, which are witnessing a rapid development in
recent years [53–70]. In particular, the tangent space ten-
sor renormalization group ( tanTRG) [71] effectively bridges
the gap between ground-state and finite-temperature TN cal-
culations. Akin to DMRG, its computational cost scales as
O(D3), where D is the bond dimension controlling the com-
putational accuracy.
In this work, we study the t-t′-J model on width-4 and -6
cylinders using the state-of-the-art thermal TN approach [63,
71, 72]. We have established the doping-temperature phase
diagram containing both t′/t > 0 and t′/t < 0 cases, and re-
veal that the d-wave SC states exist in a domelike regime on
the t′/t > 0 side (electron doping), where the SC suscepti-
bility χSC exhibits a power-law divergence. The estimated SC
temperature is as high as T ∗
c ≃ 0.15J near the optimal dop-
ing, confirming the robust d-wave SC order observed in the
ground state, which indeed corresponds to high-temperature
SC. Above the SC phase, we find the magnetic suscepti-
bility exhibits a maximum at around T ∗ that represents the
crossover temperature between the PG and high-temperature
(HT) regimes. T ∗ decreases with increasing the doping level,
resembling the temperature scale of the PG regime observed

Finite-temperature calculations have alleviated finite-size
effects, at least in the high- to intermediate-temperature
regime, where the conclusion may hold for the 2D limit. For
t′/t > 0, we have seen qualitative consistency between the
simulated W = 4 and W = 6 phase diagrams [83], where
a robust high-Tc SC regime is revealed; for t′/t < 0, on the
other hand, recently there are intensive studies on the ground-
state properties, yet the existence of SC order remains in-
conclusive [39, 46–48, 95, 96]. On the width-6 cylinder, re-
cent DMRG studies find an absence of SC but have identi-
fied the CDW order for typical doping levels of δ ≈ 1/12
to 1/8 [39, 46, 47, 95]. In our finite-temperature study, we
find the pairing correlations are indeed small [Fig. 1(b)], while

However, the pairing correlations are clearly weaker (by an
order of magnitude) compared to those for t′/t > 0. In a
different work on the t-t′-Hubbard model, coexistence of SC
order and partially filled stripe order was obtained by employ-
ing twisted-averaged boundary conditions and sampling over
low-lying states [48]. In contrast, we present a thermal aver-
age of low-energy eigenstates, characteristics of a phase rather
than an individual state. Our phase diagrams shown in Fig. 1
are thus highly indicative for wider cylinders and impose sig-
nificant constraints of the potential SC phase there. To address
the difference between current simulations and cuprate experi-
ments, it may be necessary to introduce additional terms, such
as considering a three-band model or density-assisted hop-
ping terms [97], etc. Besides, there are rapid progresses in the
cold-atom quantum simulations of Hubbard/ t-J models [98–
101], and our results provide fresh perspectives on these ex-
periments at finite temperature.

I. CONVERGENCE OF THE FREE ENERGY VERSUS BOND DIMENIONS
In Fig. S1(a), we present the calculated free energy per site of the4 × 30 (W = 4, L = 30) system. The results were obtained
by keeping two different values of D∗, i.e., D∗ = 3000 and D∗ = 4000 multiplets, respectively. The relative difference of f is

Overall, the results presented in Fig. S1 demonstrate the importance of carefully assessing the convergence of numerical results
and choosing appropriate values of D∗ to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the calculations. By verifying the convergence
of the free energy, we can also be sure that other thermodynamic quantities, e.g., the specific heat, calculated from it are also
accurate and reliable.

B. Pairing correlation contours for the width-2 and 4 systems
In the main text, we have shown the superconducting dome by the contour of pairing correlation Φyy(r) averaged over the
distances r = 3-5. Here we show that such a dome structure can also be observed in Φyy(r) at various fixed distance r. For
the 2-leg ladder, Figs. S3(a-d) shows the contour of Φyy(r) as a functions of δ and T for r = 1 -4 and a wide doping range

positive Φyy correlations. The specific boundaries on the right side of the dome can nevertheless change slightly for different
distances r, which is possibly influenced by the nearby TLL phase.
For δ > δ c, Φyy does not vanish but exhibits a vertical bar-like structure with alternating signs asδ changes. This observation
can be well explained with the dominant 2kF -oscillation in Eq. (S2), where kF = πn = π(1 − δ). As expected, in Figs. S3
we find Φyy oscillates like − cos(2πrδ) for a fixed T and r. The special SDW point δ = 1 /2 can also be seen in the finite-
temperature calculations, where the oscillation is just a π-shift, (−1)r−1, from panel (a) to (d). The amplitudes of pairing
correlations at δ = 1 /2 decay exponentially even in the ground state, due to the presence of a finite charge gap. Unlike the
SC dome for δ < δ c, these bars extent to quite high temperature T /J ≈ 1. This indicates the paring correlation described in

in the TLL phase decay very rapidly as r increases, while they are much more robust against increasing distance r in the LEL
regime under the dome.

III. MAGNETIC SUSCEPTIBILITY
In Fig. 1 of the main text, we have shown the peak temperature T ∗ of the magnetic susceptibility χm, and plot T ∗ versus
doping ratio δ in the phase diagram. Here we provide more supporting data ofχm with different system sizes and hopping ratios
t′/t. In Fig. S7 we show χm as a function of T for each (approximately) fixed δ, by tuning the chemical potential µ. In panels

fluctuations are stronger in the stripe phase, while the spin-gapped superconductivity is not favored.

IV . SPIN CORRELATIONS
In Fig. 1 of the main text, we presented a contour plot of the spin structure factor S(π, π) to illustrate the fluctuating AFM
regime. Here in Fig. S8, we provide more details of the spin structure factor S(kx, ky) as a function of kx (with fixed ky = π)
for various doping levels δ, specifically for t′/t = 0.17 in (a) and t′/t = −0.17 in (b). These data were calculated on W = 6
cylinders at a low temperature T /J = 1 /16. For both t′/t = 0 .17 and −0.17 cases, we observe that the predominant spin
correlation at low doping occurs at (π, π), indicative of AFM behavior. As the doping level increases, the (π, π) AFM peak
gets suppressed for both cases, and the peak location deviates from kx = π upon exceeding a specific doping level threshold.
The suppression of AFM peak is stronger for the t′/t = −0.17 case as compared to that of t′/t = 0.17 case. In Fig. S8(c), we
illustrate the wave vector kSDW associated with the dominant spin correlations. For t′/t = 0 .17, kSDW remains at π until the
doping level becomes greater than δ ≃ 0.17. In contrast, for t′/t = −0.17, it stays at π only up to δ ≃ 0.1. These findings
suggest that the AFM correlations are more robust in thet′/t = 0.17 case. This is consistent with experiments on cuprates where
the AFM regime is “larger” in the electron-doped side than that in the hole-doped side.
